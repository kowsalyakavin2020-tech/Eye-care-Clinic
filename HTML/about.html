1994

A practice, not a factory

Dr. Elin Varga opened one room in Rotterdam with a single promise: no patient leaves without understanding what their eyes just told us. That promise is still the whole business model.

2003

The research wing

We stopped buying instruments and started building them. A small optics lab began translating clinical questions into measuring devices — and publishing what it found, whether flattering or not.

2014

A network takes shape

We began opening clinics in cities we could staff properly, at a pace set by clinicians rather than spreadsheets. London, Singapore, São Paulo, Toronto — each one built by people who had already worked in the last.

2024

Writing the standard

Thirty years on, our protocols are taught in eleven teaching hospitals. We still calibrate every instrument by hand each morning, because a machine that drifts is a diagnosis that lies.

Our mission

To keep the world looking forward.

Sight is the sense we build our whole lives on, and the one we notice last. Our mission is unglamorous and total: find the problem earlier, explain it plainly, and fix what can still be fixed.

What we hold

Values we can actually measure.

01

Clarity before speed

No patient is rushed to keep a schedule on time. If a reading is ambiguous, we repeat it, explain it, and only then move on. A calm room produces better diagnostics than a fast one ever will.

02

Evidence over opinion

Every protocol we use is traceable to a study we are willing to name. When the evidence changes, the protocol changes. No exceptions for tradition, and none for convenience.

03

Comfort is clinical

A relaxed eye gives a truer reading. Warm rooms, unhurried voices and no fluorescent glare are not hospitality — they are part of the measurement apparatus.

04

Time is treatment

The single most effective intervention for most sight loss is catching it earlier. Every visit, however routine, is scheduled as if it might matter. Usually it does not — and then it does.

How we work

A visit that reads between the lines.

Every appointment follows the same unhurried arc, whether you are here for a routine check or a second opinion.

  • Listen first. We ask what changed before we look at anything — the history is half the diagnosis.
  • Measure twice. Each reading is taken twice on calibrated instruments before we trust it.
  • Explain plainly. You see your own scans, on your own screen, in plain language.
  • Follow through with a written plan and a named clinician you can reach again.
